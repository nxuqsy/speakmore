FOR STUDENTS WHO WANT MORE LANGUAGE CLASSES

Unlock a second language, unlock a second world

You already speak the language of memes, gaming, and group chats. Add another one and you get lyrics without subtitles, conversations with relatives who don't speak English, and shows before the dub drops. This isn't another assignment. It's a shortcut into new music, new friendships, and new opportunities, starting today, for free, without ever leaving your room.

This page breaks down why a second language is worth it, what the excuses get wrong, and exactly how to start. It also covers what to say if you want your school to offer more than one or two language classes.

01

Why bother?

Here's the honest version, not the exaggerated one: scientists are still arguing about whether being bilingual gives your brain superpowers. A 2021 meta-analysis covering more than 23,000 kids ages 3 to 17 found the "bilingual brain boost" is much smaller than people claim. Once you account for the fact that studies finding an effect get published more often than ones that don't, the boost is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

So skip the brain-hack claims. Here's what isn't up for debate:

Bilingual speakers are needed in nearly every industry, including healthcare, tech, hospitality, and finance, not just translation jobs. None of these numbers require you to be fluent by graduation. They reward any progress, because even conversational skills open doors monolingual applicants don't have.

A second language also means more shows without dubbing, more communities you can join, and more places you can travel without needing a translator app to order food. Colleges notice it too. Years of language study on a transcript signals discipline and curiosity, the same qualities admissions officers say they look for. The brain science is still being sorted out. The real-world payoff already isn't.

02

More than words

Language carries emotion, humor, and identity that translation apps flatten out. Linguist Viorica Marian, who has spent her career studying bilingual minds, describes how switching languages can change how you think, joke, and even remember your own life. A phone screen can't replicate that.

Picture this: your grandparent tells a story in their first language, the version with all the details that get lost in a secondhand translation. Or you finally get the joke your favorite streamer makes, instead of waiting for someone in chat to explain it. Or you make a friend on a game server across the world and actually get to know them, no broken translations required.

You, plus one new friend

These aren't "someday" wins. A second language makes them possible now, not after years of study. Think about the friend you've never talked to because of a language gap, or the relative you smile and nod at because a real conversation feels out of reach. A second language turns those near-misses into real connections, the kind that don't come with an ad before them.

03

"But..."

"I have Google Translate."

Translation apps are good at words and bad at everything else. They miss sarcasm, slang, and tone, the parts of a conversation that actually make it feel human. Ask anyone who's had an app mistranslate a joke into an insult.

"I'm not smart enough / it's too hard."

Nobody starts fluent. Millions of people learn through apps, shows, music, and games, stuff you already do for fun. Fluency isn't the finish line. Understanding one joke, lyric, or menu is a win on day one.

"I don't have time."

You don't need a class period. Ten minutes on an app during a bus ride, or watching one show in your target language instead of dubbed, all count. Progress builds the same way progress in a video game does: a little at a time, whenever you have a few minutes, not in one giant session.

"My school barely offers any language classes anyway."

That's a real problem, and it's fixable. Schools add or expand programs when students show up and ask for them. You don't need to wait for a class to exist before you start learning. Use free tools now, and use your voice to push for more options later, in section 04 below.

04

Your move

Download a free app

Duolingo, Memrise, or Language Reactor are all free and built for beginners. Language Reactor is especially good if you already watch shows with subtitles. It turns your favorite series into a lesson without feeling like one.

Switch your settings

Change your phone, console, or streaming app to your target language. You'll pick up common words just from using stuff you already use every day, no extra studying required.

Speak up at school

If your school offers only one or two language options, say so. Schools respond faster when students ask directly. Sign the petition below or use the email template to write to your principal or school board yourself.

Fluency starts with one setting, one show, one conversation. You don't need a classroom to start. You need ten minutes and something you already enjoy.

05

The petition

Copy this onto a sign-up sheet, a Change.org page, or a school-approved form, whatever your school allows.

Bring More World Language Options to [Your School Name]

We, the undersigned students of [Your School Name], are asking the administration to expand the world language options currently offered.

Right now, students who want to learn a language other than [current language(s) offered] don't have that option here. Learning a second language helps students connect with family, friends, and media from other cultures, and it matters for our futures. U.S. job postings seeking bilingual workers more than doubled between 2010 and 2015, and most of those jobs didn't require a college degree.

By signing this petition, we ask the school to look into adding more language course options, such as [language(s) you want offered], starting as soon as possible.
